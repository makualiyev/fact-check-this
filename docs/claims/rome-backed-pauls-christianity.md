## Steelman

Christianity did win *through* the Roman Empire. Once emperors adopted it,
imperial money, law and power made it dominant, and the Church took over
Roman structures (dioceses, the bishop of Rome, Latin). Paul, too, used
what the empire offered: Roman roads, a shared Greek language, a network of
cities, and (according to Acts) his Roman citizenship. So "Christianity
succeeded with Rome's help" has something real in it.

## Short answer

**For the first 250 years, Rome was Christianity's persecutor, not its
sponsor.**

- **Paul's own fate:** by every ancient account, Rome executed Paul himself in the 60s.
- **"Around 300":** this was the opposite of official status. In 303 Diocletian began the **Great Persecution**: churches destroyed, scriptures burned, Christians executed.
- **When support actually began:** toleration came in 311–313, imperial support with Constantine from 312, and **official state religion in 380** (Theodosius I).

Christianity grew to millions of people before any emperor helped it.

## The full picture

### Rome and the Christians: the actual timeline

| Date (CE) | Event | Rome's stance |
|---|---|---|
| 64 | After the Great Fire, Nero blames "Christians"; they are tortured and killed (Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44) | **Persecution** |
| about 64–67 | Paul dies in Rome. Every ancient account says Rome executed him (beheading, per Tertullian and Eusebius) | **Executes Paul** |
| about 112 | Pliny, governing in Asia Minor, executes Christians who won't recant. Emperor Trajan approves (*Letters* 10.96–97) | Being Christian is a capital crime |
| 250 | Decius orders everyone to sacrifice to the gods, with certificates as proof. Bishop of Rome (Fabian) killed | **Empire-wide persecution** |
| 257–260 | Valerian's persecution. Cyprian of Carthage executed | **Persecution** |
| **303–311** | **Diocletian's Great Persecution**: four edicts; churches and scriptures destroyed; clergy arrested; thousands executed | **Worst persecution ever** |
| 311 | Galerius' edict of toleration | Toleration |
| 312–313 | Constantine's conversion. The "Edict of Milan" grants toleration to *all* religions. Constantine starts funding the church | **Support begins** |
| **380** | **Edict of Thessalonica** (Theodosius I): Nicene Christianity becomes binding on all subjects | **Official state religion** |
| 391–392 | Pagan sacrifice and worship banned | Christianity enforced |

By Rodney Stark's estimate (1996), Christians numbered **about 6 million
(roughly 10% of the empire) by 300**. That growth happened before any
imperial support, and largely under the threat of persecution. Imperial
backing after 312 then made it grow much faster.

### "Rome created the Catholic Church"

No ancient source or modern historian supports this. Bishops and a church
hierarchy existed well before any emperor took an interest. Ignatius of
Antioch (about 110) already writes about bishops, and Cyprian of Carthage
(250s) about a structured church. Constantine and Theodosius did reshape the
church once they adopted it: they paid for buildings, called councils, and
enforced the Nicene creed. "Emperors shaped the church *after* the 4th
century" is defensible. "Rome created it to imprison the divine spark" is a
story with no evidence behind it.

### "Paul persecuted other Christianities with Rome's help"

Paul died around 64, more than two centuries before any Roman help existed.
His letters show him *losing* arguments as often as winning them, against
the Jerusalem leaders, rivals at Corinth, and opponents in Galatia. By his
own account he was repeatedly punished: "Three times I was beaten with
rods" (2 Corinthians 11:25), a Roman punishment.

### The kernel of truth

- **Imperial support after 312 was decisive.** It turned a large minority into the empire's religion.
- **Roman conditions helped the early spread:** peace, roads, cities, and a shared Greek language.
- **Using the empire's infrastructure isn't the same as being backed by the state,** let alone being its project.

## Thinking lesson

**Put dates on it.** The claim takes something true about the 4th century
(emperors made Christianity dominant) and moves it back onto Paul in the
1st century. Lining up the dates breaks it immediately: the "official
religion around 300" falls in the middle of the worst persecution
Christians ever faced. See [Check the timeline](../thinking/check-the-timeline.md).
