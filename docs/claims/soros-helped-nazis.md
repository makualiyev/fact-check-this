## Steelman

Soros really did describe, on *60 Minutes* in 1998, going along with a
Hungarian official while Jewish-owned property was inventoried for
confiscation. Asked if he felt guilt, he said no. He really did call 1944
among the happiest times of his life. For many viewers, a Holocaust
survivor speaking so calmly about watching Jews' property being taken was
shocking, and some commentators, not only fringe ones, criticised how he
talked about it.

## Short answer

**Misleading.** The quotes are close to real, but the story around them is
reversed.

- **Soros was the persecuted one.** He was a Jewish boy of 13–14 hiding under a false Christian name after Germany occupied Hungary in 1944, while some 430,000 Hungarian Jews were deported, most to Auschwitz.
- **What he did:** his father paid a Hungarian agriculture official to pose George as his godson. Once, he went along when that official inventoried a confiscated estate whose Jewish owners had already fled. He didn't round anyone up or take anyone's property, and there are no "logs" of him hunting Jews.
- **"Happiest time":** the remark is real, but it was about his father, who saved the family and helped others with false papers. Jones's own paraphrase ("I was with my father and I had faith in him") actually keeps that context.
- **CIA/OSS:** there's no evidence. The OSS closed in 1945, when Soros was 15 and still in Budapest. He moved to London in 1947 and to New York in 1956 as a stock trader.

## The full picture

### 1944 in Budapest

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| 19 Mar 1944 | Germany occupies Hungary. George Soros is 13. |
| May–Jul 1944 | About 430,000 Jews are deported from the Hungarian countryside, most to Auschwitz. |
| 1944 | His father, **Tivadar Soros**, a lawyer, buys false Christian identities for the family. George lives as "Sándor Kiss," the supposed godson of an official in the Ministry of Agriculture. |
| 1944 | George goes along once while the official inventories a confiscated estate. Its aristocratic Jewish owners have already left. |
| Jan–Feb 1945 | The Soviets take Budapest after a siege. The Soros family survives. |
| 1947 | Soros leaves for England, then studies at the London School of Economics. |
| 1956 | He moves to New York to work as an arbitrage trader, buying and selling the same asset in different markets to profit from price gaps. |

### The *60 Minutes* interview

In December 1998, Steve Kroft asked Soros about the estate visit. Soros said
he was 14, that he was a spectator, and that the property would have been
taken whether or not he was there ("somebody else would be taking it away
anyhow"). Asked whether he felt guilty, he said no, because he "had no role
in taking away that property."

That's the source of Jones's "if I wouldn't have done it, someone else
would have done it." But Soros wasn't saying "I did it, and so would
anyone." He was saying "I didn't do it, and my presence changed nothing."
Some critics, including mainstream writers, found the tone cold, which is a
fair opinion. Saying he "helped" is a different claim, and there's no
evidence for it. Media Matters documented how a 2006 *New Republic* column
cut the transcript to make it sound worse.

### "The happiest times of my life"

In his preface to his father's wartime memoir, *Masquerade*, Soros wrote
that it was "a sacrilegious thing to say," but those months were "the
happiest times" of his life. He explains that his father, who had survived
a Russian prison camp in the First World War, was at his best in danger: he
saved his family and helped others survive. A teenage boy in mortal danger
watching his father outwit the Nazis is the context. Jones keeps that
context in his own words ("I was with my father and I had faith in him"),
then concludes the opposite: "don't say it was happy-making."

### The other claims in the passage

- **"Tried to crash the pound":** essentially **true**. On Black Wednesday (16 September 1992), Soros's fund bet heavily against the British pound, forcing Britain out of the European exchange-rate system, and reportedly made about $1 billion.
- **"Ran the breakup of the Soviet Union":** a big overstatement. Soros funded dissidents, scholarships and civil-society groups in Eastern Europe from the 1980s. Historians credit the Soviet collapse to far larger forces. It wasn't checked in detail here.
- **"Worked for the CIA, OSS":** no evidence was found.

### Why this particular claim matters

The charge that Soros, a Holocaust survivor, was a Nazi collaborator has
circulated for years and has been widely condemned as an antisemitic smear.
Roseanne Barr publicly apologised in 2018 after tweeting it. Criticising
Soros's politics or business is a separate matter. This claim is about
what a Jewish child did while hiding from the Holocaust.

## Thinking lesson

**Read the whole quote.** Both quotes at the heart of this claim are
roughly real, which is what makes it persuasive. Read in full, "someone
else would have done it" is a denial ("I had no role"), not an admission.
"The happiest times of my life" is about a father saving his family. The
kernel is a 14-year-old's presence at one inventory. The stretch is
"helped the Nazis round up Jews."
