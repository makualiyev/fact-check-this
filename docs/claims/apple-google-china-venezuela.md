## Steelman

The claim is already fairly modest: three specific things, all of which he
says were "in the news." The strongest version is that Western tech
companies chose access to the Chinese market over their users' privacy and
free expression, and that China is exporting its surveillance model abroad.

## Short answer

**Mostly true.** All three things happened and were widely reported.

- **Apple:** in February 2018, Apple moved Chinese users' iCloud data, and for the first time the encryption keys for it, to China. The data is run by a state-owned company, as Chinese law requires. Apple says it still controls the keys, so "given to the Chinese government" overstates it, but critics warned the move put the data within the government's reach.
- **Google:** "Dragonfly" was a real Google project for a censored Chinese search app, revealed in August 2018. A prototype linked searches to users' phone numbers. It **never launched**, and Google said in 2019 it had been "terminated." It wasn't a social credit system.
- **Venezuela:** Reuters reported in November 2018 that the Chinese firm ZTE was helping Venezuela build the "fatherland card," an ID system that tracks citizens' data and is tied to food and benefits. Reuters called it "China-style social control."

The "they're going to bring it here" part is a forecast, not a fact.

## The full picture

### Apple and iCloud in China

- **What happened:** from 28 February 2018, iCloud accounts registered in mainland China were run by **Guizhou-Cloud Big Data**, a company owned by the Guizhou provincial government. Reuters reported that Apple would store the encryption keys for those accounts in China for the first time.
- **Why:** a 2017 Chinese cybersecurity law requires that Chinese users' data be stored in China.
- **The dispute:** Apple said it alone controls the keys. Security experts and human rights groups said it made little difference, since Chinese police can demand data without a court order. A 2021 *New York Times* investigation found weaker safeguards at Apple's Chinese data centres. Apple again said it keeps control of the keys.

So "given to the Chinese government" is a stretch, but it's not far from
what critics said at the time.

### Google's Dragonfly

- **Revealed:** in August 2018 by *The Intercept*, based on internal documents. Work had been under way since 2017.
- **What it was:** an Android search app that would block results China's government bans, such as "human rights" and "student protest." A prototype **linked each search to the user's phone number**, which would make monitoring easy.
- **What happened next:** employees protested and some quit. In December 2018, CEO Sundar Pichai told Congress Google had "no plans" to launch it. In July 2019 a Google vice-president told the Senate it had been "terminated."
- **"Social credit score":** China's **social credit system** is a set of government and company schemes that rate people's and businesses' trustworthiness, with penalties for bad scores. Nothing reported links Dragonfly to it. Dragonfly was about censoring and tracking search, which is bad enough but different.

When Jones spoke, in February 2019, Dragonfly was reportedly shelved but not
officially ended. Saying Google was "building" it was out of date but
understandable.

### ZTE and Venezuela's "fatherland card"

- Reuters' special report (November 2018) found that **ZTE**, a Chinese telecoms company, was working on the database and systems behind Venezuela's *carnet de la patria*.
- The card stores data such as income, property, health, benefits and party membership, and is needed for subsidised food and medicine.
- Venezuelan officials had visited ZTE in Shenzhen in 2008 to study how China tracks citizens. US senators asked for an investigation.

### The part that isn't supported: why Apple banned him

Jones links this to why Apple dropped him ("that's why Tim Cook got so mad
at me"). Apple did remove most Infowars podcasts on Sunday, 5 August 2018,
and other platforms followed the next day. That detail is accurate. But
Apple said the reason was hate speech, and Jones offers no evidence that
his China reporting was the real reason. A motive isn't evidence.

## Thinking lesson

**Credibility transfer works both ways.** It would be easy to dismiss this
because of who said it, in the same hour as claims about humanoid tissue
farms. But it checks out: three real, reported stories, slightly inflated.
Judge each claim on its own evidence, which also means giving an
unreliable speaker credit when he's right.

The one unsupported part is the motive Jones attaches ("that's why Tim Cook
got so mad at me"). A plausible motive isn't evidence of the real one.
