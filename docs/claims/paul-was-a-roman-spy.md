## Steelman

Acts, our main narrative about Paul, does present Rome favourably. Roman
officials keep finding Paul innocent, and his Roman citizenship rescues him.
Paul's conversion is known only from his own word. His travels and letters
cost real money. And a real Herodian aristocrat named Saul appears in
Josephus as a go-between who was sent to Nero. Put together, a suspicious
reader can build a story in which Paul served powerful interests.

## Short answer

**There's no evidence for it, and the reasons given don't hold up.**

- **No source says it.** No ancient source, friendly or hostile, calls Paul a Roman agent.
- **The three "mysteries" are answered in the primary sources.**
    - His conversion: Paul explains it himself.
    - His money: he worked as a tentmaker and was supported by churches and patrons.
    - "Wealthy": there's no evidence he was.
- **Rome treated him as an enemy.** He was beaten with rods (a Roman punishment) three times and jailed repeatedly, and every ancient tradition says Rome executed him.
- **It can't lose.** Adding "or maybe a double agent… spies have no loyalties" means *any* evidence fits. That's why the verdict is **Unproven** rather than False: as framed, the claim can't be tested.

## The full picture

### The three "mysteries," and their answers

| "Mystery scholars can't answer" | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Why would a **literate, wealthy** Jew join **illiterate peasants**? | Both halves are shaky. Nothing shows Paul was wealthy. He describes himself working with his hands, going hungry and in rags (1 Corinthians 4:11–12; Philippians 4:12). And Paul's churches weren't peasant groups: they were urban and socially mixed, including householders, a city official (Erastus, Romans 16:23) and patrons such as Phoebe (Wayne Meeks, *The First Urban Christians*, 1983). |
| Why did a fanatical persecutor convert? | Paul gives his own reason: he believed he had seen the risen Jesus (Galatians 1:13–16; 1 Corinthians 9:1, 15:8–9; Philippians 3:4–11). Historians can't verify a vision, but there's a large scholarly literature on this conversion (e.g. Alan Segal, *Paul the Convert*, Yale 1990; Paula Fredriksen, *Paul: The Pagans' Apostle*, 2017). "We can't verify a religious experience" is not a gap that only a spy theory can fill. |
| Where did the **money** come from? | His letters answer it. **His own trade:** tentmaking (Acts 18:3), "we worked night and day" (1 Thessalonians 2:9). **Gifts from churches:** Philippi sent help again and again (Philippians 4:15–18); "I robbed other churches by accepting support" (2 Corinthians 11:8–9). **Hosts and patrons:** Phoebe, "a benefactor of many" (Romans 16:1–2); Gaius, "host to me and to the whole church" (Romans 16:23). His scribe Tertius greets readers "in the Lord" (Romans 16:22), a fellow believer, not a hired agent. Letters were expensive, but within what a network of patrons could cover. And Paul raised money *for* the poor in Jerusalem, not the other way round (2 Corinthians 8–9). |

### How Rome actually treated Paul

- **Beaten by Roman officials:** "Three times I was beaten with rods" (2 Corinthians 11:25), the punishment carried out by attendants of Roman magistrates. Acts 16 describes one such beating at Philippi.
- **Imprisoned:** at Philippi, for two years at Caesarea, and for two more under house arrest in Rome. 1 Clement (written about 96 CE) says he was "in chains seven times."
- **Executed:** every ancient tradition (1 Clement, Tertullian, Eusebius) says Paul died as a martyr in Rome, usually dated to Nero's reign in the 60s.

Even the "Rome rescued him" episode depends on Paul's Roman citizenship,
which only Acts mentions. Some scholars think Luke, the author of Acts,
added it.

### Where the idea comes from

- **Acts is pro-Roman in tone,** which is a mainstream observation. Luke, writing decades later, seems keen to show Roman readers that Christians weren't troublemakers. That tells us about *Luke's* aims, not about who paid Paul.
- **Robert Eisenman** (*James the Brother of Jesus*, 1997) identifies Paul with "Saulus," a violent Herodian relative in Josephus who was later sent to Nero. Specialists haven't accepted the identification. Among other problems, this Saul's career runs decades after Paul's conversion.
- **Joseph Atwill** (*Caesar's Messiah*, 2005) claims Roman emperors invented Christianity. He has no relevant academic standing, and the theory isn't taken seriously by historians.
- **Ancient anti-Paul stories exist,** for example an Ebionite tale that he was a Greek who converted to marry a priest's daughter. These are smears from rival Christian groups, and none of them call him a Roman agent.

## Thinking lesson

**Beware theories that can't lose, and check the "mysteries" first.**

- **Check the "mysteries" first.** The argument starts by declaring three things unexplained, then offers a conspiracy as the explanation. Before accepting the hidden answer, check whether the "mystery" is really open. Here, all three are answered in Paul's own letters.
- **Notice when nothing could count against it.** "Paul writes like a Roman agent → he's a spy. Too blatant? → a double agent. Rome executed him? → spies have no loyalties." When every piece of evidence confirms the theory, it isn't explaining anything.
