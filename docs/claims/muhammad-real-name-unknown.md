## Steelman

The detailed story of Muhammad's life comes from Muslim biographies written
more than a century after he died, built on reports passed down orally.
Historians really do argue over how much of that story is reliable. And
"Muhammad" is a meaningful word in Arabic, roughly "the praised one."
A meaningful word can be a title, as "Christ" (*christos*, "anointed one")
is for Jesus. So isn't it possible that the name, too, is part of the later
story?

## Short answer

**No. The name is one of the best-attested facts about him.**

- **Outsiders named him early.** A Syriac Christian chronicle written around 640 calls the invading army "the Arabs of Muhammad" in a battle of 634, about 2 years after his death. An Armenian history from the 660s, the "Christian bishop" Jiang himself quotes, names him "Mahmet", a merchant-preacher.
- **Muslim sources name him too.** The Qur'an names him four times. Coins from 685–686 and the Dome of the Rock (691–692) proclaim "Muhammad is the messenger of God."

What *is* thin is the **detail** of his life, not his name or existence.
Jiang's own quoted source contradicts his conclusion.

## The full picture

### The earliest evidence for the name, in order

| Date (CE) | Source | Names him? |
|---|---|---|
| 634 (written 634–640) | ***Doctrina Jacobi***, a Greek Christian text: "a prophet has appeared with the Saracens," coming "armed with a sword" | No. "The prophet," unnamed, during the conquest of Palestine |
| written c. 640, about a battle of Feb 634 | **Thomas the Presbyter**, a Syriac Christian chronicle: a battle "between the Romans and the Arabs of Muhammad" (*ṭayyāyē d-Mḥmṭ*) near Gaza | **Yes**, within about 2–8 years of his death |
| c. 660s | **"Sebeos"**, an Armenian history (attributed to a bishop): "a certain man… whose name was Mahmet, a merchant" | **Yes.** This is the text Jiang reads aloud |
| c. 660 | **Khuzistan Chronicle**, Syriac | Reportedly yes. Wording not verified for this page |
| 7th century | **The Qur'an**: 3:144, 33:40, 47:2, 48:29 ("Muhammad is the messenger of God") | **Yes**, four times |
| 649–675 (radiocarbon) | **Tübingen Qur'an fragment** (Ma VI 165), running from sura 17 to sura 36 | Its range should include 33:40, but that leaf hasn't been confirmed |
| 685–686 | **Silver coin**, Bishapur mint (Iran): "In the name of God, Muhammad is the messenger of God" | **Yes.** The earliest *dated Muslim* object with the name |
| 691–692 | **Dome of the Rock inscriptions**, Jerusalem | **Yes**, monumentally |

So the name is attested by **hostile outsiders first**, which is the
strongest kind of evidence, since they had no reason to invent it for
him. It is attested in several languages (Syriac, Armenian, Arabic, Middle
Persian) and by people with different agendas. The founders of most ancient
religions have nothing like this. Patricia Crone, one of the most skeptical
historians of early Islam, judged the evidence that a prophet appeared among
the Arabs at this time "exceptionally good."

### What's genuinely thin (the kernel of truth)

- **The biography is late.** The first full life of Muhammad, by Ibn Ishaq (died about 767), survives only in a later edition by Ibn Hisham (died about 833). That's more than 120 years after Muhammad's death. The great hadith collections are 9th-century.
- **Details are uncertain.** Historians debate the chronology, including his birth year and the dates of events before the move to Medina (622), as well as many anecdotes, the order of the revelations, and how accurately Mecca is described.
- **No document dated to his own lifetime names him.** The oldest Qur'an leaves (Birmingham, radiocarbon-dated 568–645) do overlap his life, but they happen to contain passages that don't mention him.
- **Archaeology is limited.** Excavation at Mecca and Medina has been very restricted, as Jiang says.

But "the details of a life are uncertain" doesn't lead to "we don't know
his name." See [A thin record isn't a blank record](../thinking/thin-record-not-blank.md).

### The other details in the passage

| He says | Check |
|---|---|
| "Muhammad just means 'blessed one'" (1:44) | **Wrong translation.** It comes from the Arabic root *ḥ-m-d*, "praise": "praised one" or "praiseworthy." ("Blessed" would be *mubārak*.) It was also a real personal name in pre-Islamic Arabia, not just a title. |
| "The Quran doesn't really mention him" (0:55) | **Partly true.** He is named four times and addressed as "O Prophet" many times, but the Qur'an gives very little biography. |
| "The figure most mentioned [in the Qur'an] is Moses" (4:02) | **True.** Moses is named well over a hundred times, far more than anyone else. |
| "One of the earliest documents… a Christian bishop, 20–30 years after his death" (1:02) | **Roughly right about Sebeos, but not the earliest.** Thomas the Presbyter (c. 640) and the *Doctrina Jacobi* (634) are earlier. |
| "Hadith… just means an oral tradition" (6:30) | **Imprecise.** *Ḥadīth* means "report" or "account." These reports were passed on orally for generations, then written down. |
| "Very little archaeology… because Mecca and Medina are the two holiest sites" (13:23) | **Broadly true.** |
| Little is written because the later empire "whitewashed" his revolution (43:33) | **Not checked here.** This is his interpretation, and it would need a check of its own. Mainstream historians explain the late sources by the slow shift from oral to written culture. |

### Is anyone in the field saying the name is uncertain?

A small group, the Saarbrücken / *Inârah* school (Karl-Heinz Ohlig, Volker
Popp, "Christoph Luxenberg"), has argued since the mid-2000s that
*muḥammad* was originally a title, "the praised one," applied to Jesus.
Their evidence is mainly readings of early coins and the Dome of the Rock
inscriptions. Reviewers have disputed those readings, and mainstream
specialists treat the theory as fringe: it can't explain the 640 chronicle or
Sebeos, which describe a human Arab leader and merchant-preacher by that
name. Even the famous skeptics (John Wansbrough, Patricia Crone, Michael
Cook) never doubted that a man called Muhammad led the movement.

Jiang's "Muhammad just means blessed one" sounds like an echo of this fringe
idea, but he doesn't cite it. This is
[a consensus and a fringe](../thinking/consensus-vs-fringe.md), not a real
scholarly split.

## Thinking lesson

**A thin record is not a blank record.** "We know few details about X" is
often true. "So we don't even know *Y* about X" only follows if Y depends on
those same weak sources. Here it doesn't: the name comes from independent,
early, even hostile witnesses. And notice that the speaker's own quoted
evidence (Sebeos, "whose name was Mahmet") refutes his next sentence. **When
a speaker reads you a source, check whether it supports what they say
next.** See [A thin record isn't a blank record](../thinking/thin-record-not-blank.md).
