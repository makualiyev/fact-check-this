## Steelman

In January 2019, Virginia's governor, Ralph Northam, a children's
neurologist, was asked on the radio about a bill to loosen limits on
third-trimester abortions. Describing a woman in labour, he said the
infant would be "kept comfortable," "resuscitated if that's what the mother
and the family desired," and then "a discussion would ensue." Many people,
including senators, heard that as a doctor and the parents discussing
whether to let a *born* baby die. That's a disturbing reading, and the
governor's office didn't fully clear it up. Weeks later, the US Senate
failed to pass a bill requiring care for babies born alive after attempted
abortions. If you already distrust the abortion industry, it can look like
a pattern.

## Short answer

**False.** The quote is real, but it doesn't say what Jones says it says.

- **What Northam described** was **comfort care**, also called palliative care: the standard practice when a baby is born with a condition it can't survive. His office said he was talking only about nonviable pregnancies and severe abnormalities, which is what he says in the clip itself.
- **Killing a born baby is murder** in every US state. Federal law has defined every baby born alive as a person since 2002. No state, including Virginia, New York or California, passed a law to change that.
- **There's no evidence of organ harvesting.** The 2015 undercover Planned Parenthood videos showed staff discussing fees for donated fetal tissue. They didn't show babies kept alive, vans, Chinese facilities, or anything like $500,000 a baby. Several state investigations and a Texas grand jury found no wrongdoing by Planned Parenthood. The grand jury indicted the videos' makers instead.

## The full picture

### What Northam actually said

On 30 January 2019, on WTOP radio's *Ask the Governor*, Northam was asked
about Delegate Kathy Tran's bill. Tran had said in committee that it would
allow a third-trimester abortion even during labour if the woman's health
was at risk. The bill was tabled in committee and never passed. The clip
played on the podcast (1:03:04) has Northam saying, in part:

> "…it's done in cases where there may be severe deformities. There may be
> a fetus that's non-viable. So in this particular example, if a mother is
> in labor, I can tell you exactly what would happen. The infant would be
> delivered. The infant would be kept comfortable. The infant would be
> resuscitated if that's what the mother and the family desired, and then a
> discussion would ensue between the physicians and the mother."

The scenario he sets up is a baby with "severe deformities" or one that
is "non-viable" (can't survive). For such babies, families and doctors
really do decide together whether to attempt intensive treatment or to
provide comfort care and let the baby die naturally. That's routine in
neonatal medicine. It isn't killing.

His spokeswoman said the remarks applied only to "a woman with a nonviable
pregnancy or in the case of severe fetal abnormalities." PolitiFact rated
Trump's later claim that Northam would "execute" babies **False**. It also
noted that the governor's office didn't fully explain what the "discussion"
would decide.

### The Senate vote Jones mentions

On 25 February 2019, the Senate voted 53–44 on the **Born-Alive Abortion
Survivors Protection Act**. It needed 60 votes to get past a filibuster,
so it failed. The bill would have required doctors to give a baby born
alive after an attempted abortion the same care as any other baby of that
age, with criminal penalties. Opponents argued that existing law already
protects born babies. Supporters argued it needed enforcement. Either way,
the vote didn't make killing born babies legal. It was a vote on adding
new requirements.

### The state laws

- **New York's Reproductive Health Act** (January 2019) allows abortion after 24 weeks only when the fetus isn't viable or the woman's life or health is at risk. PolitiFact rated viral claims that it allows killing babies at birth **False**.
- **Virginia's** Tran bill died in committee.
- **California** passed no such law.

### The Planned Parenthood videos

- **What they were:** the **Center for Medical Progress** (CMP), an anti-abortion group led by David Daleiden, released undercover videos in 2015. Jones attributes them to Project Veritas. They show Planned Parenthood officials discussing payments of roughly $30–$100 per tissue specimen for medical research, which federal law allows if they only cover costs. In one, an official jokes "I want a Lamborghini." Jones's "yellow Ferrari" may be a garbled memory of that.
- **What investigations found:** several state investigations found no illegal sales. In Texas, a Harris County grand jury cleared Planned Parenthood and indicted Daleiden and a colleague; those charges were later dropped. A Republican-led House panel concluded that some affiliates profited illegally, and its Democratic members rejected that report. Planned Parenthood's own analysis said the videos were edited misleadingly.
- **What nobody found:** live-born babies kept alive for organs, mothers deceived about live births, vans to Chinese facilities, or sums like $500,000 per baby. None of that appears in the videos or any investigation.

## Thinking lesson

**Read the whole quote.** The two words Jones repeats, "kept comfortable,"
are real. The sentences around them, which Rogan's producer played on air,
set up a baby with "severe deformities" or one that is "non-viable." Read
in full, it's a doctor describing end-of-life care for babies who can't
survive. Jones's version adds the parts that make it a crime: killing, a
two-week wait, organ bidders, a price tag. None of those is in the quote.
