## Steelman

This is the claim as stated, and it doesn't need steelmanning. Jones's
broader point is that governments do morally ugly things in secret and
cover them up, and Paperclip is a real example.

## Short answer

**True.** From 1945 the US military recruited about 1,600 German scientists
and engineers. Officials rewrote some of their files to hide Nazi Party and
SS membership. Several became senior NASA figures, most famously **Wernher
von Braun**, a former SS officer whose rockets were built with concentration
camp labour and who later led the development of the Saturn V moon rocket.
NASA's own biography of him states this. What the facts *don't* support
are the bigger stories often hung on them, such as "Nazis secretly ran
NASA."

## The full picture

### What Paperclip was

- **Who and when.** Run by the US **Joint Intelligence Objectives Agency (JIOA)**. It began in 1945 as "Operation Overcast," and recruitment ran mainly into the late 1950s.
- **The rule, and how it was broken.**
    - President Truman formally approved the programme in September 1946.
    - The directive excluded anyone who had been more than a nominal Nazi Party member or an active supporter of Nazism.
    - The JIOA got around this by **rewriting security files**, removing or downplaying Party and SS memberships. Journalist Linda Hunt's 1985 investigation documented this.
- **Scale:** about 1,500–1,600 people, plus families.
- **Why:** to win the technology race and keep this expertise away from the Soviet Union.

### The NASA connection

| Person | Nazi-era record | NASA role |
|---|---|---|
| **Wernher von Braun** | Nazi Party member 1937. SS officer from 1940, reaching the rank of major. Led the V-2 rocket programme. NASA's biography says he was aware of conditions and involved in decisions on slave labour at the Mittelwerk factory. | Director of Marshall Space Flight Center 1960–70, chief architect of the Saturn V |
| **Arthur Rudolph** | Nazi Party member. Operations director for V-2 production at Mittelwerk. | Project director of the Saturn V. In 1984, investigated by the Justice Department's Office of Special Investigations, he left the US and gave up his citizenship rather than face proceedings. |
| **Kurt Debus** | Member of the SA, the Nazi Party and the SS. V-2 flight-test director. | First director of Kennedy Space Center, 1962–74 |
| **Hubertus Strughold** | Aviation medicine researcher linked to human experiments | The "father of space medicine." The space-medicine award named after him was retired in 2013 over his past. |

### The human cost behind the rockets

The V-2s were built in tunnels at **Mittelbau-Dora** by concentration camp
prisoners. About 60,000 prisoners passed through, and more than 20,000 died
(US Holocaust Memorial Museum). The V-2 killed more people building it than
it did as a weapon.

### Context

- **Not only the US.** In October 1946 the Soviet Union deported more than 2,000 German specialists and their families in a single night (Operation Osoaviakhim). Britain ran its own recruitment too.
- **The moral debate is real.** One side says Cold War necessity and the Moon landing justified it. The other says it let people complicit in slave labour escape justice, with records deliberately falsified. Both arguments are made by serious historians.

### What this fact does *not* show

Conspiracy storytelling often uses a true fact like this as a foundation
for much bigger claims:

- **"Nazis secretly ran NASA / the US government."** No evidence. The recruits were technical staff under US military and civilian control, and NASA's leadership and policy were American.
- **"All the Paperclip scientists were Nazis."** No. Many were Party members, fewer were SS, and some were neither.
- **"Tens of thousands of Nazis."** No. Paperclip totalled about 1,600 people.
- **"It's still being hidden."** No. It has been public since the 1980s, and NASA's own biographies describe it.

## Thinking lesson

**Some conspiracies are real, and that's exactly why they're powerful.**
Paperclip was a secret government programme with falsified records, so
"conspiracy" is the right label and "true" is the right verdict. Someone
who tells you a true, shocking story earns credibility, and that
credibility then carries over to their next claim, which may be false. The
same speaker who was right about Paperclip spent years claiming the Sandy
Hook shooting was staged. Judge each claim on its own evidence. See
[Credibility transfer](../thinking/credibility-transfer.md).
