## Steelman

US government doctors really did deliberately infect people with syphilis
and gonorrhoea, without their knowledge, and the government formally
apologised for it. At Tuskegee they let hundreds of Black men go untreated
for decades to watch the disease progress. The same doctor was involved in
both, and in prison experiments at home. When an institution has done that
and hidden it, it's reasonable to suspect the record we know about is
incomplete.

## Short answer

**Misleading.** The kernel is real and horrifying, but almost every detail
is inflated or wrong.

- **How many:** the known US experiments that deliberately exposed people to syphilis or gonorrhoea involved **a few thousand people at most**: about 1,300 in Guatemala and a few hundred US prisoners. Not "over a million."
- **Tuskegee:** Rogan's "conventional story" is the correct one. The 399 men with syphilis were **already infected** when the study began in 1932. The crime was withholding treatment, even after penicillin became the standard cure in the late 1940s, until 1972.
- **Guatemala, 1946–48:** here US doctors *did* infect people on purpose, to test whether penicillin could **prevent** infection. It was prevention research, not a vaccine. It came to light only in 2010.
- **Dates and places:** the experiments span 1932–72, not 1947–83. No US syphilis experiment in Peru was found. Jones may mean Guatemala.

## The full picture

### What actually happened

| Study | When | Who | What was done | Deliberate infection? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tuskegee**, Alabama | 1932–1972 | 600 Black men: 399 with syphilis, 201 without, as a comparison | Treatment withheld so doctors could study the disease's natural course. Men were told they were being treated for "bad blood." | **No**, but treatment was withheld for decades |
| **Terre Haute** federal prison, Indiana | 1943–44 | Prisoner volunteers, paid $100 and a letter to the parole board | Exposed to gonorrhoea to test preventive treatments. It mostly failed to infect them, so the work moved abroad. | Yes (gonorrhoea) |
| **Guatemala** | 1946–48 | About 1,300 people exposed, including prisoners, soldiers, psychiatric patients and sex workers. Only about 700 are recorded as treated. | Infected with syphilis, gonorrhoea or chancroid to test penicillin as prevention | **Yes** |
| **Sing Sing** prison, New York | 1953–54 | About 60 prisoner volunteers | Tested a vaccine made from killed syphilis bacteria, then exposed them to the disease | Yes, in vaccine research |

The same Public Health Service doctor, **John Cutler**, worked on Terre
Haute, Guatemala, Sing Sing, and later Tuskegee.

### How each came to light

- **Tuskegee:** a Public Health Service employee, Peter Buxtun, raised concerns for years and finally gave documents to the press. AP reporter Jean Heller broke the story on 25 July 1972, and the study ended that November. President Bill Clinton formally apologised in 1997.
- **Guatemala:** historian Susan Reverby found Cutler's records in the University of Pittsburgh archives and published them in 2010. Secretary of State Hillary Clinton and Health Secretary Kathleen Sebelius apologised, and President Obama called Guatemala's president. A presidential bioethics commission investigated and called it "unconscionable." It found that 83 subjects died during the period, though it couldn't say how many deaths the experiments caused.

So "it's a cover" gets it backwards. The Tuskegee account Rogan described
is the documented one, and the hidden experiment (Guatemala) was exposed by
a historian working in an archive, and admitted by the government.

### The "vaccine" angle

Jones presents this under "vaccines" as a Trojan horse. Of these studies,
only Sing Sing tested a vaccine, on about 60 prisoner volunteers. Guatemala
tested whether penicillin could prevent infection. Tuskegee tested nothing.
It just watched. None of it involved giving people vaccines that secretly
contained syphilis.

### Why it still matters

These experiments are a real reason for distrust. Studies have linked the
Tuskegee revelations to lower trust in medicine among Black Americans. The
history is bad enough as documented. Inflating it to "over a million" and
"still going on" adds nothing but makes it easier to dismiss.

## Thinking lesson

**Find the kernel, then measure the stretch.** Something like this really
happened, and it's worse than many people know, which is why my prior was
so high. But the claim as stated is a million victims, vaccines, 1947–83,
and a cover story about Tuskegee. Against the record it's roughly 1,300 deliberately
infected in Guatemala and a few hundred in prisons, prevention research,
1932–72, and the Tuskegee story is accurate. A dark true story is the best
carrier for a bigger false one. Put dates and numbers next to it before
deciding how far it reaches.
