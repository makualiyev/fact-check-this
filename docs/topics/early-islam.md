# The sources for early Islam

Muhammad died in 632 CE. Within about a century his followers ruled from
Spain to Central Asia. What evidence survives from that century falls into
four groups:

| Kind | Examples | Earliest | What it's good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-Muslim texts** | *Doctrina Jacobi* (Greek), Thomas the Presbyter, Khuzistan Chronicle (Syriac), "Sebeos" (Armenian) | 634–660s | Independent, outside confirmation of the conquests, of an Arab prophet, and of the name Muhammad |
| **The Qur'an** | Manuscript leaves at Birmingham, Tübingen, Sana'a and elsewhere, radiocarbon-dated to the 7th century | Some within decades of his death | The text itself, which is early, but has little biography |
| **Documents and objects** | Papyri (PERF 558, 643 CE), rock graffiti, coins (from 685 with the name), Dome of the Rock (691) | 640s–690s | Dated, physical evidence of the early state and its beliefs |
| **Muslim histories** | *Sīra* (biography): Ibn Ishaq (d. about 767) via Ibn Hisham (d. about 833); al-Wāqidī; hadith collections such as al-Bukhārī (d. 870) | From the mid-8th century | Nearly all the detail of Muhammad's life, but written down 120+ years later from oral reports |

Some useful terms:

- **Sīra:** biography of the Prophet.
- **Ḥadīth:** a "report" of something Muhammad said or did. Each report carries a chain of transmitters (*isnād*).
- **Hijra:** the move from Mecca to Medina in 622, year 1 of the Islamic calendar.

**The scholarly debate** is mostly about the fourth group: how much of
the late biographical tradition preserves real memory. Positions range
from **"largely reliable at its core"** (e.g. Schoeler and Görke's work on
reports going back to ʿUrwa ibn al-Zubayr, died 712) to **"heavily shaped
by later concerns"** (Goldziher and Schacht on hadith; Wansbrough; Crone &
Cook's *Hagarism*, 1977). Almost no specialists doubt the core: that
Muhammad existed, was called Muhammad, and led the movement in Arabia in
the early 7th century.

**Good starting points:** Patricia Crone's short essay
[What do we actually know about Mohammed?](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/mohammed_3866jsp/)
and Robert Hoyland's *Seeing Islam as Others Saw It* (1997).
