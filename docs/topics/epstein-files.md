# The Epstein files

Jeffrey Epstein was a New York financier convicted in 2008 in Florida of
procuring a minor for prostitution. He was arrested again in July 2019 on
federal sex-trafficking charges and died in a Manhattan jail cell in August
2019. The New York City medical examiner ruled it suicide. He knew a huge
number of powerful people, so anything that comes out of his records gets
attention.

## What was released, and when

| When | What |
|---|---|
| November 2025 | Congress passes the **Epstein Files Transparency Act** and Trump signs it. The Justice Department (DOJ) must publish its unclassified records on the Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell investigations. |
| December 2025 | The DOJ starts releasing in batches, beginning with thousands of pages. The slow, piecemeal rollout draws criticism. |
| 30 January 2026 | The big release: more than **3 million pages**, about 180,000 images and 2,000 videos. Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche says that ends the review. Total released: about 3.5 million pages. |
| 2026 | The House Oversight Committee questions people named in the files, including Bill Clinton (February) and Bill Gates (June), behind closed doors. |

The documents carry **Bates numbers** starting with `EFTA` (a page ID
stamped on each document). Citing one lets anyone find the exact page.

## How to read a document from the files

1. **A name in the files isn't an accusation.** The files contain contact lists, flight logs, invitations and emails from people who turned Epstein down. The DOJ and news outlets repeat this caveat for a reason.
2. **Ask who wrote it.** A large part of the material is Epstein's own: his emails, notes and **drafts, including emails he sent to himself**. These show what Epstein claimed, wanted or planned to say. They aren't findings about what other people did. Example: the 2013 "dear Bill" letter about Gates (EFTA00965766), which Epstein wrote in his adviser's voice and mailed to his own address. See [the check](../claims/gates-syphilis-epstein-party.md).
3. **Was it sent?** A draft that never left his outbox tells you about Epstein, not about the person it describes.
4. **Remember what he used people for.** Epstein collected information about powerful people and, by several reports, used it as leverage. A document that damages someone may have been written *to* damage them.
5. **Go to the page, not the summary.** Most viral claims come from news write-ups, and those from other write-ups. Unofficial "Epstein data" sites and wikis with their own "grades" aren't the DOJ archive.
