# How the Bible was put together

A **canon** is the official list of books a community treats as scripture.
The Bible wasn't written as one book. It's a library that different
communities assembled over centuries, and their lists still differ.

- **The Jewish Bible (Tanakh)** has three parts:
    - **Torah**, the five books of Moses. Largely fixed by around the 5th–4th century BCE.
    - **Nevi'im**, the Prophets.
    - **Ketuvim**, the Writings. The last part to settle, by around the 2nd century CE.
- **The Septuagint** was a Jewish translation into Greek made in Alexandria from the 3rd century BCE. It included some extra books. Early Christians, mostly Greek-speaking, used it as their Bible.
- **The New Testament** formed between the 2nd and 4th centuries.
    - Paul's letters and the four Gospels were widely accepted early.
    - Books like Revelation and Hebrews were argued over longer.
    - Athanasius's Easter letter of 367 is the first list that matches today's 27 books exactly.
- **Why the lists still differ:**
    - Catholics and Orthodox kept the extra Septuagint books in their Old Testament. The Catholic list was fixed at the Council of Trent in 1546.
    - Protestant reformers went back to the Hebrew canon and moved those extra books to an "Apocrypha" section, or dropped them.
    - The Ethiopian church kept an even larger list, including 1 Enoch and Jubilees.

A useful habit: when someone says "the Bible says," ask **which Bible**, in
**which translation**, from **which manuscript tradition**.
