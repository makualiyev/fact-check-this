# The first Christians

- **The earliest evidence is written by insiders, decades after the events.**
    - Paul's letters date from about 50–60 CE.
    - The Gospels date from about 66–100 CE.
    - Non-Christian mentions (Josephus, Tacitus) are brief and later.
    - So historians work with the sources critically: who wrote this, when, for whom, and what did they want to prove?
- **It started as a Jewish movement.**
    - Jesus' followers were Jews who believed he was the Messiah and had risen from the dead.
    - The first community, in Jerusalem, was led by Peter and Jesus' brother James.
- **The big early argument** was whether non-Jews who joined had to become Jews: circumcision and food laws. Paul's answer, no, won out, which shaped everything after.
- **Separation was slow.** "Christianity" and "Judaism" became clearly separate religions gradually, over generations and arguably centuries. Historians call this the "parting of the ways."
- **Good free starting point:** Dale B. Martin's [Introduction to the New Testament](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-152) (Open Yale Courses).
