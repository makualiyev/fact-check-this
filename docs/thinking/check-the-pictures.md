# Check what the pictures show

**The pattern:** a short video plays someone talking while images cut in
on every few words: a red circle on a statue, an arrow pointing at a
detail, a glowing brain. Each picture seems to *confirm* the sentence it
appears under. But the editor chose the images after the fact. They may be
mislabelled, cropped, from a different place or century, or not show the
thing at all. Because they go by in a second or two, there's no time to
look.

**The habit:** pause the video and ask of each image:

1. **What is it actually?** A museum object, a modern replica, a wax
   figure, a stock illustration, a screenshot from a meme?
2. **Where and when is it from?** Look for a museum label, a watermark or
   a reverse-image search. A Facebook watermark means it came from a post,
   not a collection.
3. **Does it show what the voice says?** A red circle tells you where to
   look, not what you're seeing. Is the circled thing really a pine cone,
   a symbol, the same object?
4. **Does a picture of X prove the claim about X?** Even an accurate
   picture of a pine cone on a relief shows *that* it's there, not *what it
   meant*.

**Example:** a Short about the Vatican's pine cone puts a red circle on a
decorative knob of a pope's staff, and shows a carving of a child holding
grapes while the voice talks about pine cones in ancient art. See
[the check](../claims/pine-cone-symbolizes-pineal-gland.md).
