# Watch the story change as it's retold

**The pattern:** a story starts out hedged and specific: *someone* claimed
*something* in *some document*, and it was denied. Each retelling changes
it in a predictable way. In 1947 the psychologists Gordon Allport and Leo
Postman described three of these changes in *The Psychology of Rumor*.
They had people pass a description of a picture down a chain, one person
to the next:

- **Levelling.** Details drop out, and the story gets shorter and simpler. The first things to go are often the caveats: who said it, that it's unconfirmed, that it was denied.
- **Sharpening.** A few details are kept and exaggerated. A vague term turns into the most vivid specific one.
- **Assimilation.** The story bends toward what listeners already expect. In their experiment, a truck in the picture clearly labelled "TNT" was often remembered as an ambulance carrying "medical supplies".

Later research qualified this: rumors sometimes get *more* accurate when
people can check them against each other. That's why the versions have to
be compared rather than assumed.

Two more things help a changed story spread:

- **The passing mention.** "Let's not dwell on the fact that…" (rhetoricians call it *paralipsis*) states a claim while waving it away, so nobody stops to ask for a source.
- **The dropped attribution.** "X claimed that Y happened" becomes "Y happened". This is the most important change, and the easiest to miss.

**The habit:**

1. **Find the version nearest the source.** The document, the original article, the first post. See [Start with the earliest source](earliest-source-first.md).
2. **Put the versions side by side.** What fell away (hedges, who said it, denials)? What was added (a named disease, a place, a number)?
3. **Distrust detail that appears late.** Detail feels like evidence, but detail that appears downstream was added by someone who didn't have the source.
4. **Ask what the expected story is.** If the new detail makes the story fit a familiar picture better, that's assimilation at work.

**Example:** Epstein's unsent 2013 draft, written to himself, claimed Gates
had caught "an STD". By the time Sergey Minaev mentioned it on YouTube, it
had become "Gates was infected with syphilis at one of these parties",
told as a fact. See [the check](../claims/gates-syphilis-epstein-party.md).
