# Secret framing

**The pattern:** a well-known fact is presented as forbidden or suppressed
knowledge. "What they never taught you." *Secret History.* The facts may be
true. The framing is what's misleading: it implies a cover-up, and it makes
*you* feel like an insider.

**Why it matters:** the "hidden truth" feeling carries over to the next
claim in the series, which may not be true at all. See
[Credibility transfer](credibility-transfer.md).

**The habit:** ask **"Is this actually hidden?"**

- Is it in Britannica, a standard textbook, a museum, or an official biography?
- If yes, it's not a secret. It's just new *to you*. That's great, but it isn't evidence the speaker has special access.
- If it really was hidden once (like Paperclip), when did it become public, and who exposed it?

**Examples:** the Old Testament's share of the Bible is textbook material
([check](../claims/christian-bible-mostly-jewish-bible.md)). Paperclip was
secret once, but has been public since the 1980s and is on NASA's own
website ([check](../claims/operation-paperclip-nazis-at-nasa.md)).
