# Parallels aren't proof

**The pattern:** "Jesus is just a copy of [Dionysus / Horus / Mithras]," or
"this ritual is really that ancient rite." Lists of similarities feel
convincing because our brains love patterns. The biblical scholar Samuel
Sandmel had a name for the overdone version: **"parallelomania"** (1962).

**The habit:** test each parallel on four questions:

1. **Is it accurate?** Check the original myth, not a summary of a summary. Many viral parallels are simply wrong.
2. **Is it early enough?** A source written *after* the thing it supposedly inspired can't be its source.
3. **Is it specific?** Wine, meals, special births and gods who suffer are everywhere in ancient religion. Generic overlaps prove nothing.
4. **Is there contact?** Does the supposed borrower show any knowledge of the source, or opposition to it?

Similarities are worth studying. Comparing religions is a real field. But
"similar" is a starting question, not a conclusion.

**Example:** "Dionysus was born of a virgin, killed and resurrected, and
eaten in a meal that became the Eucharist." See [the check](../claims/paul-copied-dionysus.md).
