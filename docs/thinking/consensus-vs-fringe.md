# Consensus vs. fringe

**The pattern:** a speaker presents a minority scholarly view, or a
19th-century provocation, as if it were established fact, or as the truth
"they" don't want you to know.

**The habit:** before deciding, ask three questions:

1. **What do most specialists think?** Look at textbooks, encyclopedias like Britannica, and university course materials. They summarise the mainstream.
2. **Who holds the other view, and why?** Minority views can be right; science moves that way sometimes. But they need *more* evidence, not less.
3. **Is this a real split or a lone voice?** "Scholars disagree" means informed experts are genuinely divided. One famous author against a field is not a split.

**Example:** that Paul shaped Christianity is consensus. "Paul invented it"
is a minority thesis (Maccoby, 1986). See
[the check](../claims/paul-invented-christianity.md).
