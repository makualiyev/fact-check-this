# A motive isn't evidence

**The pattern:** a claim points to something that *would give* a person a
reason to act secretly: a business interest, a debt, a friendship, a
location. Then it treats that reason as if it showed the act. "He has a
factory in China, so he's a Chinese spy."

**The habit:** split it into three questions:

1. **Is the motive or exposure real?** Often it is, and it's worth taking seriously.
2. **What would we expect to see if the secret act were happening,** and what would we see if it weren't? An open business interest predicts open lobbying for that business. Espionage predicts something different: secret tasking, hidden contacts, leaks.
3. **Is there any evidence of the act itself?** Reporting, charges, documents, defectors. If the only evidence is the motive, the case hasn't started.

The motive question isn't useless. Conflicts of interest are real, and
journalists and governments examine them for good reason. But "could be
pressured" and "is an agent" are different claims, and need different
evidence.

**Example:** Tesla's Shanghai factory gives Beijing real leverage over Elon
Musk. It's not evidence he's a spy. See
[the check](../claims/elon-musk-chinese-spy.md).
