# Thinking toolkit

Reusable habits for judging claims. Each one came out of a real case and
links back to the claims where it mattered.

<!-- auto:list -->
- [A thin record isn't a blank record](thin-record-not-blank.md) — "We know little about X" doesn't mean "we know nothing about X." Ask which facts rest on the weak sources and which have independent support.
- [Base rates](base-rates.md) — Before being impressed by a correct prediction, ask how likely it was anyway, and whether it could have failed.
- [Check the expertise](check-the-expertise.md) — Who is speaking, and is this their field? Charisma and confidence are not credentials.
- [Check the timeline](check-the-timeline.md) — Put dates next to every step of a story. Many confident claims collapse when the events can't have happened in that order.
- [Consensus vs. fringe](consensus-vs-fringe.md) — Find out where experts actually stand before deciding. A minority view isn't automatically wrong, but it needs extra evidence.
- [Credibility transfer](credibility-transfer.md) — A speaker who is right about one surprising thing borrows credibility for the next. Judge each claim on its own.
- [Kernel of truth](kernel-of-truth.md) — Most misleading claims grow from something real. Find the kernel, then measure how far the claim stretches it.
- [Parallels aren't proof](parallels-arent-proof.md) — "X looks like Y, so X copied Y" needs accurate, early, specific parallels and a sign of contact. Otherwise it's pattern-matching.
- [Secret framing](secret-framing.md) — "Hidden history" packaging makes ordinary textbook facts feel like revelations, and lends that glow to whatever follows.
- [Start with the earliest source](earliest-source-first.md) — Before accepting a new story about the past, find the oldest account. A late source, or a modern guess, has to explain why it knows better.
- [Theories that can't lose](theories-that-cant-lose.md) — If every possible piece of evidence confirms a theory, it isn't explaining anything. Also check whether the "mysteries" it solves are really open.
- [What's the denominator?](what-is-the-denominator.md) — A percentage means nothing until you know what it's a percentage of.
