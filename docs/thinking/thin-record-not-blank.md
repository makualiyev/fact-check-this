# A thin record isn't a blank record

**The pattern:** a speaker correctly says the sources on some person or
event are sparse, late or biased, then quietly slides to doubting things
that rest on *different, stronger* evidence. "The biographies are late,
so we don't even know his name."

**Why it works:** "historians admit we know little" sounds humble and
sophisticated. It makes a sweeping doubt feel like the cautious position.

**The habit:** sort the facts by **what they rest on**:

1. **Which sources are actually weak?** Late, second-hand, or written by people with an agenda.
2. **Does the specific fact in question depend on them?** Or is it confirmed by early, independent, ideally *hostile* witnesses, or by objects such as coins, inscriptions and documents?
3. **Did the speaker's own evidence say otherwise?** Speakers sometimes quote a source that contradicts their next sentence.

Facts usually come in tiers: a strong core (the person existed, their name,
roughly when and where), a middle layer (major events) and a soft outer
layer (anecdotes, exact dates, speeches). Doubt should be applied tier by
tier, not all at once.

**Example:** Muhammad's detailed biography is 120+ years late, but his
name is in a Christian chronicle from about 640. See
[the check](../claims/muhammad-real-name-unknown.md).
