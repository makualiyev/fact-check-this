# Check the expertise

**The pattern:** a compelling speaker talks across many fields (geopolitics,
ancient history, theology, economics) with the same confidence in each.

**The habit:**

1. **What is this person actually trained in, and what do they do for a living?** Titles like "Professor" or "expert" can be self-chosen.
2. **Is this claim inside or outside that field?** Expertise doesn't transfer. A great teacher of English isn't thereby an authority on early Christianity.
3. **Does it matter here?** Credentials don't make claims true or false. Evidence does. But outside their field, a speaker's claim needs checking against people inside it.

This is not about dismissing people without credentials. It's about knowing
how much weight their *say-so* carries before you've checked.

**Example:** [Jiang Xueqin](../people/jiang-xueqin.md), "Professor" by his
own styling, is a high-school teacher with a degree in English literature,
lecturing on the origins of Christianity.
