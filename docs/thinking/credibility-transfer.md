# Credibility transfer

**The pattern:** someone tells you something shocking that turns out to be
true. You're impressed, and you lower your guard for whatever they say
next. A mix of true revelations and false claims is far more persuasive
than false claims alone.

**Why it works:** we judge *sources*, not just claims, because checking
everything is exhausting. A source that surprised us with a truth feels
like it has access to hidden knowledge.

**The habit:**

1. When a claim shocks you, check *it*, not the speaker's general reputation.
2. Keep a separate tally per claim. Being right about A is weak evidence about B, especially if B is in a different field.
3. Ask: is this speaker mixing documented history with speculation? Where exactly does one turn into the other?

**Example:** [Operation Paperclip](../claims/operation-paperclip-nazis-at-nasa.md)
is true, and the same speaker spent years claiming a real school shooting
was staged ([Alex Jones](../people/alex-jones.md)).
