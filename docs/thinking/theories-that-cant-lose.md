# Theories that can't lose

**The pattern:** a conspiracy theory usually comes in two moves:

1. **Manufacture a mystery.** "Scholars can't explain X, Y and Z."
2. **Offer the hidden explanation.** "The only answer is that he was working for someone."

Then, when evidence turns up against it, the theory stretches to absorb it:

- "He looks like an agent." → *He's an agent.*
- "Isn't that too obvious?" → *Then he's a double agent.*
- "But they executed him." → *Spies have no loyalties.*

**Why it works:** a theory that explains everything *feels* powerful. But a
theory that no possible evidence could contradict tells you nothing. The
philosopher Karl Popper called this being **unfalsifiable**.

**The habit:**

1. **Check the mystery first.** Is X really unexplained, or does the primary source answer it? Often it does.
2. **Ask: what would prove this wrong?** If the speaker can't name anything, or keeps moving the target, the theory is unfalsifiable. Judge it as **Unproven**, not as clever.
3. **Ask: where is the positive evidence?** A document, a witness, a payment. "It would make sense if…" is not evidence.

**Example:** "Paul was clearly a spy, or maybe a double agent." See
[the check](../claims/paul-was-a-roman-spy.md).
